# Step 2

## Step 2: Identifying the performance obligation in a contract

The process of identifying performance obligation requires an entity to determine whether it promises to transfer either goods or services that are distinct, or a series of distinct goods and services that meet certain conditions. There promises may not be limited to those explicitly included in written contracts.

## Analyzing whether identified promises represent performance obligations

Guidance

A promise is classified as a performance obligation, if the good or service is capable of being distinct  distinct (ASC 606-10-25-19 (a)) and distinct in the context of the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19 (b)). (ASC 606-10-25-14)

**Criterion 1**
Can the customer benefit from the goods or services (e.g. good or service could be used, consumed, sold for an amount that is greater than scrap value) on its own or together with other readily available resources (a readily available resource is a good or service that is sold separately (by the entity or another entity) or a resource that the customer has already obtained from the entity (including goods or services that the entity will have already transferred to the customer under the contract) or from other transactions or events) (ASC 606-10-25-19(a))?

**Criteria 2**
Is the Company promise to transfer the good or service separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19(b))?? The following factors should be considered with regard to the above question

i   Significant integration service: the Company provides a significant service of integrating the good or service with other goods or services promised in the contract into a bundle of goods or services that represents the combined output for which the customer has contracted.

ii  Significant modification/customisation: Goods or services significantly modify or customise another good or service promised in the contract.

iii  Highly dependent/highly interrelated: Goods or services of the Company are highly dependent on, or highly interrelated with, other goods or services promised in the contract

Possible values of "Conclusion":
- C1 - "Series of distinct performance obligation as below mentioned criteria is met:-
  1. The goods or services are substantially the same.
  2. Each distinct goods or service is a performance obligation satisfies over time.
  3. The same method i.e (input) would be used to measure progress toward satisfaction of each distinct goods or services in the series."
- C2 - Promise is a distinct performance obligation
- C3 - Bundle of goods or services is a single performance

Key evidence highlights supporting C1 for Phone, Chat, PSO:
- The Work Order sets defined service streams with scope and hours/languages for continuous operations (e.g., Front Office hours for Phone/Chat and Back Office hours for PSO) indicating stand-ready, recurring services over the term .
- Fees are structured as Fixed Service Charges by service line/location with monthly invoicing across the 5-year term, evidencing a consistent pattern of transfer over time; SLAs and service credits are measured monthly (Exhibit B), supporting use of a consistent method to measure progress for each service stream  .
- Transition/Transformation are separately funded milestones but are setup/enabling activities that do not transfer a distinct good/service to the customer independent of the ongoing services; Transformation from 2025 is stated to be vendor-invested, further indicating it is an enabler of service delivery rather than a separately sold deliverable .

Notes:
- Optional rate card-based services (Agency Services, WFM, Quality, Reporting) are available on a call-off basis at stated annual FTE rates (Tab 2.b) and invoiced when deployed; no preferential pricing or material right is indicated at contract inception, so these options are not performance obligations unless and until exercised in future orders .

## Conclusion
Overall Conclusion: The contract contains three performance obligations, each accounted for as a **series of distinct services (C1)** satisfied over time: (1) **Unlicensed inbound Phone support**, (2) **Unlicensed Chat support**, and (3) **PSO back-office processing** (scope per Section 5(c) and Exhibit B SLAs)   . All other promises (Transition, Transformation, Intake/Triaging, technology/telephony enablement, BCP/DR, governance/training/QA/SOPs) are not separate performance obligations under ASC 606; they are setup, enabling, or fulfillment activities that are integrated into the delivery of the BAU services and therefore included in the bundled service delivery to support these series performance obligations    . For completeness, the Work Order also includes a vendor-paid signing bonus to the customer, which is consideration payable and not a promise to transfer goods/services (not a performance obligation) .
