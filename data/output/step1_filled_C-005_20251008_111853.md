# Step 1: Identifying contract with the customer

## A. contract exists if

i. Collection is considered probable

ii. It has commercial substance

iii. Rights to goods and services and payment terms can be identified

iv. It is approved and the parties are committed to their obligations

An ASC 606 contract exists for revenue recognition purposes once collectibility is determined to be probable; additional credit assessment evidence is required.

## B. Combination of contracts

Accounted for as a single contract if one or more of the following criteria are met

• Contracts were negotiated as a single commercial package;

• Consideration in one contract depends on the other contract; or

• Goods and services are a single performance obligation

### Analyzing combination contracts

- Question: Are there any contracts entered into at or near the same time (rebuttable presumption 3 months) with the same customer or related parties of the customer?

- Answer: **Yes.** The DocuSign certificate for this execution indicates an accompanying “Data Protection Information Security Agreement (FEC)” was included in the same envelope as the Framework Services Agreement, executed in the same time window (March 30–April 1, 2024) . The Data Protection and Information Security Agreement content is included among the provided documents and cross-references the Framework Services Agreement exhibits and confidentiality framework, evidencing a coordinated execution package .

#### Is there a combination of contracts necessary (606-10-25-9) ?

- Question: Contracts were negotiated as a package with a single commercial objective (e.g. contract would be loss-making without taking into account the consideration received under another contract)?
  - Answer: **Yes.** The Data Protection and Information Security Agreement sets security and confidentiality obligations that apply “pursuant to each applicable Statement of Work” and specifically references the Agreement’s exhibits (e.g., Exhibits N, O, P), indicating it was negotiated alongside the Framework Services Agreement as part of a single outsourcing relationship and compliance framework .

- Question: Consideration in one contract depends on price or performance of the other contract (e.g. failure to perform under one contract affect the amount paid under another contract)?
  - Answer: **No.** The consideration and payment mechanics are contained in the Framework Services Agreement (Sections 22 and 23). The Data Protection agreement establishes process/security requirements but does not introduce separate pricing or interdependent consideration mechanisms that alter the amounts payable under the main agreement  .

- Question: Goods or services promised in the contracts (or some goods or services promised in each contract) are a single performance obligation in accordance with paragraphs 606-10-25-14 through 25-22 ?
  - Answer: **No.** The Data Protection and Information Security Agreement largely comprises obligations that enable and govern how the services are delivered (security, confidentiality, access controls), rather than constituting separate promised goods or services transferred to the customer. The Framework Services Agreement defines the services and performance obligations via SOWs and service levels; the security agreement supports, but does not itself create, distinct deliverables to the customer  .

If one or more of the above questions are answered with "Yes", the contracts should be combined.

Conclusion

Given the coordinated execution and shared commercial objective, the Data Protection and Information Security Agreement should be viewed as part of the same overall contract package with the Framework Services Agreement for ASC 606 purposes. Practically, the Data Protection agreement is ancillary and incorporated by reference into the service arrangement, so combining does not change transaction price or performance obligations as defined in the Framework Services Agreement and SOWs  .

Remarks

The combination of contracts is largely formal because the Data Protection agreement is an enabling/ancillary document to the Framework Services Agreement executed in the same DocuSign envelope; there is no separate consideration stream to allocate. All substantive pricing, invoicing, and scope are already captured within the Framework Services Agreement and its SOWs  .

## Final Conclusion

Based on the attached documents, the Framework Services Agreement between ABC Global Services (UK) International Limited (Provider) and XYZ Music Entertainment (Customer) is a valid, executed arrangement with identifiable rights, clearly stated payment terms, and commercial substance, supported by SOWs and exhibits. However, to conclude that a contract exists for ASC 606 revenue recognition, the provider must also assess whether collection from the customer is probable. The provided documents do not include customer credit information; therefore, while criteria (a)–(d) are met, criterion (e) (probable collectibility) is **Unknown** based solely on the files. Once collectibility is assessed as probable, the arrangement (inclusive of the Data Protection agreement executed in the same package) can be accounted for as a single contract under ASC 606, with revenue recognition then proceeding in accordance with the Framework Services Agreement and applicable Statements of Work    .
