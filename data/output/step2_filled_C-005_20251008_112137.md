# Step 2: Identifying the performance obligation in a contract

The process of identifying performance obligation requires an entity to determine whether it promises to transfer either goods or services that are distinct, or a series of distinct goods and services that meet certain conditions. There promises may not be limited to those explicitly included in written contracts.

## Analyzing whether identified promises represent performance obligations

Guidance

A promise is classified as a performance obligation, if the good or service is capable of being distinct (ASC 606-10-25-19 (a)) and distinct in the context of the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19 (b)). (ASC 606-10-25-14)

- Criterion 1
Can the customer benefit from the goods or services (e.g. good or service could be used, consumed, sold for an amount that is greater than scrap value) on its own or together with other readily available resources (a readily available resource is a good or service that is sold separately (by the entity or another entity) or a resource that the customer has already obtained from the entity (including goods or services that the entity will have already transferred to the customer under the contract) or from other transactions or events) (ASC 606-10-25-19(a))?

- Criteria 2
Is the Company promise to transfer the good or service separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19(b))? The following factors should be considered with regard to the above question:
  i. Significant integration service
  ii. Significant modification/customisation
  iii. Highly dependent/highly interrelated

Notes on conclusions and evidence:
- The core promise is to deliver outsourced BPO services for F&A and ISB “Towers,” under a BPO SOW with defined Service Levels and monthly invoicing (Services §§3.1–3.2; BPO SOW/SLAs; §23) — a stand‑ready managed service provided continuously over the term, i.e., a series of distinct services that are substantially the same, satisfied over time, and measured using the same method (time/units) consistent with the Agreement’s unit-based pricing and SLAs (Objectives §1.1; SLAs; invoicing)     .
- Transition activities are set‑up in nature (provider-funded; no BPO Transition charges payable; standby no‑charge if provider delays), enabling the transfer of the ongoing service but not transferring a separate benefit on their own (§§4.1–4.5) .
- Reporting, SLAs/credits, governance/change control, data protection/security, and DR are attributes or supporting activities inseparable from the ongoing managed service, and do not constitute separate promised goods or services (Sections 6, 9; Exhibits D, E, L; DP/IS Agreement)    .
- Termination/Expiration Assistance is a contingent, future service to be provided (and compensated) only upon termination/expiration (§§31.12, 32.1–32.4); it is not a promised transfer at inception and is therefore not a current performance obligation  .
- “Projects” and “New Services” become distinct only when specifically approved and contracted via a separate SOW or change order; until then, they are options, not current promises (§§3.12, 3.17)  .

## Conclusion
Overall Conclusion

Based on the contract documents, the arrangement contains a single performance obligation comprising a series of distinct services: the stand‑ready, ongoing BPO managed services across the defined Towers (F&A BPO and ISB BPO) to be delivered over time under the BPO SOW and SLAs (Services §§3.1–3.2; Exhibits B.1(a)–B.1(b)) — Conclusion C1. All other promises (initial Transition, SLAs/credits, reporting, governance/change control, security/data protection, disaster recovery) are setup/supporting activities or attributes of the core service and are not separate performance obligations. Termination/Expiration Assistance and any Projects/New Services will be evaluated if and when they are added by SOW or triggered at termination; they are not current POs in the transaction price at inception (Sections 4, 6, 9, 15; 23; 31–32; 3.12–3.17; Exhibits)     .
