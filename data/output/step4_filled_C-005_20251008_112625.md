# Step 4: Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations in the contract

The transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation - generally each distinct good or service - to depict the amount of consideration to which an entity expects to be entities in exchange for transferring the promised goods or services to the customer.

An entity generally allocates the transaction price to each performance obligation in proportion to its stand-alone selling price. However, when specified criteria are met, a discount or variable consideration is allocated to one or more, but not all, performance obligations.

This step of the revenue model comprises two sub-steps that an entity performs at contract inception.

1. Determine stand-alone selling prices

2. Allocate the transaction price

Step 4: Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations in the contract

**Question:** Allocation of transaction price required? 

**Answer:** **Yes.** Rationale: The arrangement contains a single performance obligation—a stand‑ready series of managed BPO services across the F&A and ISB “Towers,” delivered under the BPO SOW and SLAs (Services §§3.1–3.2; Exhibits B.1(a)–B.1(b))—so allocation is trivial: 100% of the transaction price is assigned to that one performance obligation. Observable prices for the services are provided via the Charges and Resource Baselines exhibit (Exhibit B.1(c)), which sets unit-based rates intended to reflect “market” unit costs (Objective 1.1) for ongoing monthly invoicing of services    .

**Allocation of variable consideration**

**Question:** Is there an allocation of a variable consideration ? (ASC 606-10-32-39 to 32-41)?

**Answer:** **No.** Rationale: There is only one performance obligation (the stand‑ready managed BPO service), and the contract’s variable consideration—such as SLA Performance Credits under the Service Level Agreements and usage/volume-based adjustments (e.g., Extraordinary Events; RRCs/ARCs)—relates to the overall delivery of that single service series rather than to a separate performance obligation. Accordingly, no allocation among multiple POs is required; variable amounts are estimated and recognized within the single PO consistent with the SLA/charges mechanics (Service Levels and Exhibit B.1(b); Sections 39–40; Exhibit B.1(c))   . 

Additional note: If new, distinct services are later added via approved SOWs under Section 3.17 (New Services) or Projects under Section 3.12, those would be evaluated at that time with their own SSPs and allocations; at contract inception, only the single managed BPO service PO exists for allocation purposes  .
