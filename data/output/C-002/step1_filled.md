# Step 1: Identifying contract with the customer

## A. contract exists if

i. Collection is considered probable

ii. It has commercial substance

iii. Rights to goods and services and payment terms can be identified

iv. It is approved and the parties are committed to their obligations

### Existence of a contract

| Description | Yes/No | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Is the contract approved and the parties are committed to their obligation ((ASC 606-10-25-1(a) ) ? | **Yes** | The agreement is marked “Execution Version” and states “This Agreement is made on 3 July 2024 between” Pacific International Lines (Customer) and WNS Global Services (UK) International Limited (Supplier), evidencing a finalized contract. The agreement also contains a “Counterparts” clause enabling execution in counterparts, and Supplier warranties that execution, delivery and performance have been properly and duly authorized and are enforceable, indicating approval and commitment by the parties.    |
| Are each parties' rights regarding the goods or services to be transferred identifiable (ASC 606-10-25-1(b)) ? | **Yes** | The scope of services is expressly defined (Transition Services, Transformation Implementation Services, Transformation Recurring Services, Technology Implementation Services, Technology Recurring Services, and Operational Services), and the agreement and schedules detail roles/responsibilities (RACI) and specific process deliverables (e.g., Bookings, Export Documentation). This makes the parties’ rights and obligations over the services identifiable.    |
| Can the payment terms for the goods and services be identified (ASC 606-10-25-1(c)) | **Yes** | Payment terms are specified, including invoicing mechanics and due dates. For example, Transformation/Technology Recurring Service Charges are invoiced by the 14th day of the month following the “Invoice Month,” with undisputed amounts due no later than the 45th day after the end of the Invoice Month; Operational Service Charges are invoiced monthly. Some prices are invoiced in equal monthly installments per Schedule 5. One-time charges and timing are also illustrated.   |
| Has the contract commercial substance (ASC 606-10-25-1(d)) ? | **Yes** | The contract provides for significant, ongoing services with specified pricing and invoicing and sets out the customer’s commercial objectives (e.g., cost efficiencies, productivity enhancements). The presence of monetary consideration (e.g., recurring charges, one-time charges) and mechanisms such as the Upfront Monetisation Sum repayment on termination indicate commercial substance.    |
| Is the collection of consideration probable ("likely") (ASC 606-10-25-1(e)) ? | **Unknown** | The contract establishes enforceable payment terms and obligations, but it does not provide evidence of Customer credit assessment or other information necessary to conclude collectability. While terms require payment and set due dates, the contract itself does not include explicit creditworthiness evaluation, so a probability assessment would require management’s separate credit analysis outside the contract.  |

Conclusion: Based on the contract documents, criteria (a)–(d) are met. Criterion (e) (collectability) cannot be concluded from the contract itself and requires management’s credit assessment. Subject to that assessment concluding collectability is probable, a contract exists between the parties under ASC 606.

## B. Combination of contracts

Accounted for as a single contract if one or more of the following criteria are met

• Contracts were negotiated as a single commercial package;

• Consideration in one contract depends on the other contract; or

• Goods and services are a single performance obligation

### Analyzing combination contracts

**Question:** Are there any contracts entered into at or near the same time (rebuttable presumption 3 months) with the same customer or related parties of the customer?

**Answer:** **Yes.** The agreement states that, in conjunction with entering into this Agreement, the Supplier shall enter into Implementation Agreements with the Customer or the Customer’s designated Affiliates for specified countries. These Implementation Agreements are executed “in conjunction with” this Agreement and are considered separate agreements that generally mirror the main agreement with territory-specific modifications. Affiliates constitute related parties of the customer.

#### Is there a combination of contracts necessary (606-10-25-9) ?

**Question:** Contracts were negotiated as a package with a single commercial objective (e.g. contract would be loss-making without taking into account the consideration received under another contract)?

**Answer:** **Yes.** The Implementation Agreements are contemplated “in conjunction with” the main agreement and consist of the same terms (with necessary local modifications), reflecting a unified commercial arrangement for multi-country service provision under a single Transition and Transformation Plan. This indicates a single commercial objective across the package. 

**Question:** Consideration in one contract depends on price or performance of the other contract (e.g. failure to perform under one contract affect the amount paid under another contract)?

**Answer:** **Yes.** Pricing/true-up mechanisms refer to Operational Service Charges “under all Statements of Work and Implementation Agreements” when calculating Corrected Amounts and productivity true-ups. This cross-referencing means consideration under one agreement depends on amounts under others, evidencing interdependence. 

**Question:** Goods or services promised in the contracts (or some goods or services promised in each contract) are a single performance obligation in accordance with paragraphs 606-10-25-14 through 25-22 ?

**Answer:** **Unknown.** While the services are integrated under a common plan and common tools, the agreement contemplates separate Statements of Work and territory-level Acceptance Certificates, indicating potentially distinct deliverables by territory. Determining whether they form a single performance obligation requires assessment of whether services are “highly interdependent or interrelated” across territories; the contract structure alone is inconclusive.  

If one or more of the above questions are answered with "Yes", the contracts should be combined.

**Conclusion**

Given that at least one criterion is met (negotiated as a package with a single commercial objective and pricing dependencies across agreements), the related Implementation Agreements entered into in conjunction with the main agreement should be combined for ASC 606 purposes.  

**Remarks**

- The combination conclusion is driven by explicit linkage between the main agreement and Implementation Agreements and the cross-contract pricing/true-up mechanisms referencing “all Statements of Work and Implementation Agreements.” 
- While combination is indicated, determining whether promises across territories constitute a single performance obligation requires further analysis of the nature of the services and interdependencies beyond what is evident in the contract text.  

## Final Conclusion

- A contract exists under ASC 606 provided that management concludes collection is probable based on credit assessment. The contract is approved, has commercial substance, identifies rights and payment terms, and evidences parties’ commitment.    
- Related Implementation Agreements executed in conjunction with the main agreement should be combined due to a single commercial package and cross-contract consideration dependencies.
