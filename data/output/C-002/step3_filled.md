# Step 3: Determining transaction price

The ‘transaction price’ is the amount of consideration to which the Company expects to be entitled in exchange for transferring goods or services to a customer, excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties.

## A. Cash consideration

- Question: Is the consideration or a part of the consideration fixed? 
  - Answer: **Yes.** The contract specifies fixed pricing components, including:
    - Fixed annual FTE Rates per Supplier Site and Process for Operational Services (billed monthly) in Schedule 5, paragraph 4.1–4.2 (table of FTE rates) .
    - Fixed annual “Prices” for Transformation Recurring Services and Technology Recurring Services, invoiced in equal monthly installments (e.g., DSOP and IA support/hosting) in Schedule 5 and Appendices 5-4/5-5 .
    - One-Time Charges for Transition/Transformation/Technology Implementation, which become “Earned One-Time Charges” upon milestone acceptance and are invoiced per the Invoice Month mechanics in Schedule 5, paragraph 9.2 and the illustrative invoice example  .

- Question: Is the consideration or a part of the consideration variable (e.g., liquidated damages, discounts, standby rates, etc.) (ASC 606-10-32-5 to 32-9)? 
  - Answer: **Yes.** The arrangement contains several variable elements:
    - Service Level Credits for Minimum Service Level Defaults, capped at an “At Risk Amount” (10% of aggregate Operational Services Charges for the month), per Clause 12.10 and Schedule 4 (Service Levels) with detailed methodology and example  .
    - Productivity Guarantee true-ups (Corrected Amount/Ad Hoc Corrected Amount) payable by either party within 60 days, with no interest, per Clause 22.4.3 and Schedule 25 (Periodic Productivity Commitment Table)  .
    - Volume-based Additional Resource Charges and Reduced Resource Credits (ARC/RRC) calculated against agreed baselines (Schedule 5, paragraph 4.5 with formulas and illustrations) .
    - Potential “one-time benefit” tied to achieved/actual net savings if the Customer elects to implement Supplier-proposed cost-saving solutions (a gain-share style mechanism) per Clause 22.2 .

- Question: Is there a valid expectation arising from business practices, published policies or specific statements that the consideration is less than the price stated in the contract representing a variable consideration (ASC 606-10-32-7a)?
  - Answer: **No.** Reductions arise from explicit contractual mechanisms (Service Level Credits, ARC/RRC formulas, and Productivity Guarantee true-ups) rather than unstated business practices or implied concessions   .

- Question: Are there other facts and circumstances indicating that the Company intention was to offer a price concession to the customer representing a variable consideration?
  - Answer: **No.** Payment terms are firm (generally the 45th day after the end of the Invoice Month) with interest on late undisputed amounts, and credits are formulaic, not discretionary concessions  .

### If the previous questions were answered "Yes" please describe the facts and circumstances and specify why the criteria mentioned above are met

- Service Level Credits: The contract directly links credits to shortfalls against Critical Service Levels with an “At Risk Amount” cap of 10% of monthly fees (Schedule 4; Clause 12.10), making these amounts variable consideration that reduce the transaction price when probable and estimable  .
- Productivity/efficiency initiatives: The Periodic Productivity Commitment Table sets caps on billable FTEs over time and requires True-Up payments (Corrected Amount/Ad Hoc Corrected Amount) within 60 days, no interest, reflecting variability in amounts based on actual productivity and FTE banding (Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 25)  .
- Volume variability: ARC/RRC formulas adjust consideration when actual volumes deviate more than 15% from baselines (Schedule 5, paragraph 4.5) .
- Gain-share style one-time benefit: If Customer implements a Supplier-proposed cost-saving solution, Supplier earns a one-time benefit based on actual net savings (Clause 22.2); this is contingent and variable until the savings occur and are agreed .

### Please describe the approach to estimate the variable amount for each variable consideration (most likely amount, expected value) and explain why the method used better predicts the consideration to which the Company will be entitled

| Variable consideration | Method | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Service Credit | Expected Value Method | Multiple Critical Service Levels can default in a month, each with an allocation and an overall cap; a probability-weighted portfolio estimate best reflects the monthly outcome pattern, constrained to the 10% At Risk Amount per Schedule 4 and Clause 12.10  . |
| Earnbacks | Not applicable | No “earn-back” mechanism is specified; therefore, no estimation is required for earnbacks . |
| Gain Share | Most Likely Method | The outcome is typically binary (Customer implements or does not) with a contractually defined one-time benefit tied to actual net savings; selecting the most likely amount captures the contingent, single-outcome nature when inclusion is not constrained (Clause 22.2) . |
| Improvement Initiatives | Expected Value Method | For Productivity Guarantee true-ups (Corrected Amounts) and ARC/RRC, variability depends on actual productivity, band transitions and volume variances across many processes and months; a probability-weighted estimate aligns to the formulaic calculations and frequent resolution (Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 5 para 4.5; Schedule 25)   . |

### Please explain the reasonableness whether it is highly probable that a significant reversal in the amount of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur (ASC 606-10-32-11)

- For Service Level Credits, ARC/RRC, and Productivity True-Ups, amounts are:
  - Determined by explicit formulas, measured monthly, and resolved quickly (within 60 days for true-ups), which reduces the duration of uncertainty and the magnitude of potential reversals (Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 5 para 4.5; Schedule 4)   .
  - Constrained operationally (e.g., At Risk Amount cap of 10% for Service Level Credits) further limiting reversal risk .
- Gain-share amounts are constrained until the Customer elects to implement and actual net savings are realized/quantified per Clause 22.2. They should be included only when the underlying contingency is resolved or the constraint is lifted, making a significant reversal unlikely .

### Please consider the following factors, which could increase the likelihood or the magnitude of a revenue reversal (ASC 606-10-32-12)

- Question: Is the amount of consideration highly susceptible to factors outside the Company’s influence (e.g., movement of a market or an index, subsequent sales of a good or service of the customer)? 
  - Answer: **Yes (partially).** Some variable elements depend on factors outside the Supplier’s control (e.g., Customer’s actual volumes for ARC/RRC and Customer’s decision to implement cost-saving solutions for gain-share) (Schedule 5 para 4.5; Clause 22.2)  .

- Question: Does the Company expect that the uncertainty about the amount of consideration is not expected to be resolved for a long period of time? 
  - Answer: **No.** Service Credits and ARC/RRC resolve monthly; Productivity Guarantee true-ups are paid within 60 days; these short intervals reduce long-duration uncertainty (Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 5 para 4.5; Schedule 4)   .

- Question: Have the Company limited experience or other limited evidence with similar types of contracts resulting in limited predictive value? 
  - Answer: **No.** The contract’s mechanisms are formulaic/deterministic (defined baselines, caps, and calculation methods), which enables reliable estimation even absent historical experience (Schedule 5 para 4.5; Schedule 4; Clause 22.4.3)   .

- Question: Does the Company have a practice of offering price concessions or changing payment terms of similar contracts in similar circumstances? 
  - Answer: **No.** Payment terms are specified (generally 45th day after the end of the applicable month) with late-payment interest on undisputed balances (Schedule 5 paras 9.2.4, 9.3.3, 9.4.2; Clause 15.7.2; Paragraph 9.6)   .

- Question: Has the Company a practice of either offering a broad range of price concessions or changing the payment terms and conditions of similar contracts in similar circumstances? 
  - Answer: **No.** There is no evidence in the contract of broad discretionary concessions; credits/adjustments are defined by contractual formulas and caps (Schedule 4; Schedule 5)  .

- Question: The transaction price consists of variable consideration(s) which are deemed probable and need(s) to be included in the transaction price.
  - Answer: **Yes.** Include expected Service Level Credits (subject to the 10% At Risk cap), expected ARC/RRC adjustments, and Productivity Guarantee true-ups when estimable and unconstrained; defer gain-share until Customer approval and savings are realized/quantified under Clause 22.2    .

## Conclusion

- The transaction price comprises fixed fees (FTE-based Operational Service Charges; fixed-price recurring services; earned one-time implementation charges) and variable elements (Service Level Credits, ARC/RRC, Productivity Guarantee true-ups, and contingent gain-share). 
- Apply the constraint: include only those variable amounts for which it is highly probable that a significant revenue reversal will not occur (e.g., routine monthly credits/charges and near-term true-ups), and defer contingent gain-share until the contingency is resolved (Clause 22.2; Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 4; Schedule 5)    .

## B. Non-cash consideration

- Question: Does the consideration include a non-cash component?
  - Answer: **No.** Consideration is stated and invoiced in USD, with no non-cash consideration identified (Schedule 5 invoicing/payment provisions; allocation of fees) .

## C. Consideration payable to a customer

Guidance: Consideration payable to a customer includes cash amounts paid or credits that reduce amounts owed.

- Question: Is there any consideration payable to a customer (ASC 606-10-32-25 to 32-27)?
  - Answer: **Yes.** 
    - Service Level Credits reduce amounts payable (or are paid) to Customer, capped at the At Risk Amount (Schedule 4; Clause 12.10)  .
    - Unused credits or rebates must be paid to Customer within 30 days of expiration/termination if no further fees are due (Clause 15.6.7) .
    - Note: The “Upfront Monetisation Sum” is paid by Supplier to Customer but is repayable in fixed installments (non-interest bearing) and subject to immediate repayment on termination; it functions as an interest-free financing arrangement, not a price concession (Clauses 3.3.1–3.3.6)  .

Consideration Payable to customer:
- Service Level Credits (contra revenue).
- Any unused credits/rebates due at termination (contra revenue when related to fees).
- Upfront Monetisation Sum is outside ASC 606 transaction price as it is repayable financing, not consideration for services (Clauses 3.3.1–3.3.6)  .

- Question: Does the consideration payable to a customer (or to the customer’s) represent a payable for a distinct good or service?
  - Answer: **No.** Service Level Credits/unused rebates are reductions of the consideration for services, not payment for distinct goods/services. The Upfront Monetisation Sum is a separate financing arrangement repayable by the Customer, not payment for a distinct good/service  .

### Conclusion

- Reduce the transaction price for Service Level Credits and any similar credits/rebates. 
- Treat the Upfront Monetisation Sum as a separate financing arrangement (outside the ASC 606 transaction price), given the fixed, non-interest-bearing repayment schedule and termination repayment requirements (Clauses 3.3.1–3.3.6)  .

## D. Significant financing component

- Question: Is the period between the transfer of promised goods and services to a customer and the customer payment less than one year (also relevant when the customer pays before transfer) (ASC 606-10-32-18)?
  - Answer: **Yes.** For service invoices:
    - One-Time Charges: undisputed amounts due no later than the 45th day after the end of the Invoice Month (Schedule 5, paragraph 9.2.4) .
    - Recurring Charges: invoices issued by the 14th day of the month following the Invoice Month; payment due by the 45th day after the end of the Invoice Month (Schedule 5, paragraphs 9.3.2–9.3.3) .
    - Operational Service Charges: invoiced by the 14th day each month for the prior month; payment due by the 45th day after month-end (Schedule 5, paragraphs 9.4.1–9.4.2; Clause 15.7.2)  . 
  - Therefore, the practical expedient applies to the revenue streams.

If one of the following questions are answered with "No", no indication for a financing component exists (practical expedient):

- Question: The customer paid for the goods or services in advance, and the timing of the transfer of those goods or services is in the discretion of the customer.
  - Answer: **No.** Services are invoiced after transfer (or contemporaneously for the Invoice Month), not paid significantly in advance at the Customer’s discretion (Schedule 5) .

- Question: A substantial amount of the consideration promised by the customer is variable, and the variability is not substantially within the control of the customer or the entity.
  - Answer: **Yes.** There is substantive variability (Service Credits, ARC/RRC, productivity true-ups), but variability does not in itself create a financing component and these resolve within short intervals (monthly/within 60 days) (Clause 22.4.3; Schedule 5 para 4.5; Schedule 4)   .

- Question: Differences between the promised consideration and the cash selling price arises for other reasons other than the provision of finance to either the customer or the entity.
  - Answer: **Yes.** Differences chiefly arise from performance-based metrics (Service Levels), productivity commitments, and volume variances rather than financing terms (Schedule 4; Clause 22.4; Schedule 5 para 4.5)   .

Please specify why the criteria mentioned above are met:
- Invoice-to-payment cycles are ~45 days; true-ups settle within 60 days; these are well within one year, supporting use of the practical expedient for significant financing components on revenue (Schedule 5; Clause 22.4.3)  .
- Variable elements are operationally driven and formulaic; not financing-related.

- Question: A significant financing component exists for which the entity needs to account
  - Answer: **No (for revenue streams).** Payments occur within 45–60 days, so the practical expedient applies. Note, however, the contract contains a separate, non-interest-bearing financing arrangement—the **Upfront Monetisation Sum** paid by Supplier to Customer and repaid over months 24–45, or immediately upon termination (Clauses 3.3.1–3.3.6). This should be treated as a financing arrangement outside the ASC 606 transaction price (i.e., not as consideration for goods/services)  .

Notes and additional evidence supporting the above:
- Invoicing/payment timing and late-payment interest on undisputed invoices are clearly defined (Schedule 5; Clause 15.7; 9.6 interest clause)   .
- True-ups specify “no interest shall be payable” and that payment “shall not constitute a late payment,” further evidencing that these are operational adjustments rather than financing components (Clause 22.4.3) .
