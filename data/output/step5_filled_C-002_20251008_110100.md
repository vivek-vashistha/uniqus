## Analyzing whether identified promises represent performance obligations

Guidance

A promise is classified as a performance obligation, if the good or service is capable of being distinct (ASC 606-10-25-19 (a)) and distinct in the context of the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19 (b)). (ASC 606-10-25-14)

Criterion 1
Can the customer benefit from the goods or services (e.g. good or service could be used, consumed, sold for an amount that is greater than scrap value) on its own or together with other readily available resources (a readily available resource is a good or service that is sold separately (by the entity or another entity) or a resource that the customer has already obtained from the entity (including goods or services that the entity will have already transferred to the customer under the contract) or from other transactions or events) (ASC 606-10-25-19(a))?

Criteria 2
Is the Company promise to transfer the good or service separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19(b))?? The following factors should be considered with regard to the above question

i   Significant integration service: the Company provides a significant service of integrating the good or service with other goods or services promised in the contract into a bundle of goods or services that represents the combined output for which the customer has contracted.

ii  Significant modification/customisation: Goods or services significantly modify or customise another good or service promised in the contract.

iii  Highly dependent/highly interrelated: Goods or services of the Company are highly dependent on, or highly interrelated with, other goods or services promised in the contract

## Selecting a method that depicts the performance i.e. a pattern of transfer of control of the goods and services to the customer

Guidance when performance obligation is satisfied over time.

According to ASC 606 either output or input related methods can be applied consistently in order to measure the progress (ASC 606-10-25-32) , if a reasonable estimate is possible (ASC 606-10-25-36 to 25-37)

Method 1: Output: Progress is measured based on the value of goods and services transferred to the customer to date (e.g., surveys/appraisals of work performed, achievement of service milestones, or time elapsed within the service period). For this contract, pure output measures (e.g., units of transactions completed) would not fully capture all in‑period efforts and could exclude work in process.

Method 2: Input: Progress is measured based on the Company’s efforts toward satisfying the performance obligation (e.g., resources consumed, labour hours expended, costs incurred, time elapsed). For this contract, inputs correlate well with consideration: Operational Services are priced and invoiced based on monthly FTE rates (pro‑rated as needed) and recurring service “Prices” are billed monthly, aligning revenue with the Supplier’s level of effort each period   .

*Guidance: Limitations on applying the units-of-delivery or units-of-production methods*

An output method based solely on units delivered (e.g., number of transactions) would not faithfully depict performance because it may omit significant in‑process effort and control transferred within a reporting period. The contract’s mechanics demonstrate continuous service delivery with monthly invoicing and service‑level assessments, including pro‑ration for partial months and credits applied to the month’s services, indicating that recognizing revenue based on inputs (time/FTEs) better reflects the pattern of transfer and avoids excluding work in process controlled by the customer in the period  .
