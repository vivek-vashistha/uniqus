# Step 5: Recognize revenue when (or as) each performance obligation is satisfied

An entity recognizes revenue when or as it satisfies a performance obligation by transferring a promised service to the customer. For this contract, the single performance obligation identified in Step 2 is the stand‑ready BPO managed services across the F&A BPO and ISB BPO Towers delivered under the BPO SOW and SLAs.

Performance obligations according to Step 2 | Criterion 1 | Criterion 2 | Criterion 3 | Conclusion
---|---|---|---|---
Stand‑ready BPO managed services across F&A BPO and ISB BPO Towers (BPO SOW with SLAs and monthly invoicing) | **Yes** | **No** | **Not applicable** | **Over time**

- Rationale for Criterion 1 (simultaneous receipt and consumption): The Provider invoices within five calendar days after each month‑end for “Services provided in the preceding calendar month,” with payment due net 30, demonstrating that XYZ Music receives and consumes the benefits as the services are performed each month . The services are delivered continuously under Statements of Work with Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and defined charges/resource baselines, confirming a managed services model with ongoing monthly consumption and measurement .
- Rationale for Criterion 2: The managed BPO services operate XYZ Music’s back‑office processes; they do not create or enhance a customer‑controlled asset as it is created. They are delivered on a service basis under SLAs rather than producing a discrete asset under construction for the customer .
- Note on Criterion 3: This criterion is not required because Criterion 1 is met. For completeness, the Agreement does require payment of undisputed charges for services “satisfactorily performed … up to and including the effective date of termination,” and obligates continuation of services during termination assistance periods, but the performance does not create an asset with an alternative use; therefore Criterion 3 is not determinative here  .

Question: Performance obligations satisfied over time?  
Answer: **Yes.** The core managed BPO services are provided and consumed continuously, with monthly in‑arrears invoicing for the immediately preceding month’s services and governance through SLAs, indicating transfer over time under ASC 606‑10‑25‑27(a)  .

Question: Performance obligation satisfied at a point in time  
Answer: **No.** There is no single point at which control transfers; instead, services are performed and consumed throughout each month and billed in arrears, consistent with over‑time recognition for a stand‑ready service series .

## Selecting a method that depicts the performance i.e. a pattern of transfer of control of the goods and services to the customer

Guidance when performance obligation is satisfied over time.

According to ASC 606, either output or input methods can be applied consistently in order to measure progress (ASC 606‑10‑25‑32), if a reasonable estimate is possible (ASC 606‑10‑25‑36 to 25‑37).

Method 1: Output: Progress is measured based on the value of services transferred to the customer to date. For this contract, suitable output measures include time‑elapsed by service period (monthly) and/or units of service delivered under the unit‑based pricing set out in the Charges and Resource Baselines to the BPO SOW, with services governed by SLAs  .

Method 2: Input: Progress is measured based on the Provider’s efforts (e.g., resources consumed, labor hours), but given the contract’s explicit unit‑based pricing and monthly service cadence, output measures (time/units) more directly align to how consideration is earned and billed under the SOW and invoicing provisions  .

Performance Obligation satisfied over time | Method used | Description
---|---|---
Stand‑ready BPO managed services across F&A BPO and ISB BPO Towers | Output method (time elapsed / units of service) | Recognize revenue over time using an output measure that mirrors the contract’s economics: (i) time‑elapsed for the monthly service period billed in arrears for “Services provided in the preceding calendar month,” and/or (ii) units of service delivered at the unit‑based rates in the BPO Charges and Resource Baselines. SLAs govern service outcomes, and any performance credits reduce consideration in the period of occurrence consistent with variable consideration guidance  .

Guidance: Limitations on applying the units‑of‑delivery or units‑of‑production methods

An output method would not provide a faithful depiction if the selected output omits services for which control has already transferred. For example, relying solely on “units processed” could under‑reflect performance in months where significant stand‑ready capacity and SLA‑governed responsiveness are provided but volumes are temporarily lower. Given the managed‑services nature, a time‑elapsed measure (monthly service period) combined with unit‑based measurement where appropriate best reflects performance and aligns with how charges are structured and invoiced under the Agreement’s SOW exhibits and monthly invoicing terms  .

Additional contract evidence consistent with over‑time service delivery:
- The Agreement’s objectives explicitly contemplate unit‑based pricing and consumption flexibility for ongoing BPO services, supporting an output‑based pattern of transfer tied to time/units of service delivered .
- SLAs and step‑in/credit mechanics govern monthly service performance and remedies, reinforcing a recurring, period‑by‑period measurement of performance and consideration .
- Upon termination, the Agreement requires payment of undisputed charges for services performed to date and requires continued services during a defined termination/expiration assistance period at the then‑current service levels, consistent with a continuous service model recognized over time  .
