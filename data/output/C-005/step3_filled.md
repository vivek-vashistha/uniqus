# Step 3: Determining transaction price

The ‘transaction price’ reflects the consideration ABC Global Services (UK) International Limited (Provider) expects to be entitled to for providing the BPO Services to XYZ Music Entertainment, considering fixed and variable elements specified in the Framework Services Agreement and incorporated exhibits.

## A. Cash consideration

- Question: Is the consideration or a part of the consideration fixed?  
  - Answer: **Yes.** The Agreement provides rate structures and charge mechanics through the Charges and Resource Baselines exhibits to the Statements of Work (e.g., Exhibit B.1(c) for the BPO SOW) that set out fixed rate cards/unit rates for services to be invoiced monthly; invoicing and net 30-day payment terms are specified in Section 23.1 (General) (invoice within 5 calendar days after month-end; payment due on the 30th day from invoice), and invoices must include specified detail per Section 23.2 (Invoice Summary)  .

- Question: Is the consideration or a part of the consideration variable (e.g., liquidated damages, discounts, standby rates etc.) (ASC 606-10-32-5 to 32-9)?  
  - Answer: **Yes.** Variable elements include (a) Service/Performance Credits linked to SLA outcomes (referenced in Service Levels and “At‑Risk Amount” constructs) and a one‑time credit waiver carryover item at March 31, 2024 (Section 3.18), (b) volume/usage variability via unit-based pricing and Reduced Resource Credits (RRCs)/Additional Resource Credits (ARCs) and Extraordinary Event pricing adjustments (Sections 39–40), (c) Most‑Favored Pricing automatic retroactive price reductions/refunds (Section 22.2), and (d) potential benchmarking charge adjustments upon agreement (Section 7.2)     .

- Question: Is there a valid expectation arising from business practices, published policies or specific statements that the consideration is less than the price stated in the contract representing a variable consideration (ASC 606-10-32-7a)?  
  - Answer: **Yes (contractual).** Specific mechanisms reduce price under defined conditions: Most‑Favored Pricing automatically and retroactively lowers charges/refunds differences if Provider offers materially better pricing to named comparators (Section 22.2); SLA Performance Credits reduce amounts due when service levels are not met (Service Levels/Exhibit B.1(b)); Extraordinary Event pricing and RRCs/ARCs can adjust charges based on volume/market events (Sections 39–40)   .

- Question: Are there other facts and circumstances indicating that the Company intention was to offer a price concession to the customer representing a variable consideration?  
  - Answer: **No.** The Agreement does not evidence discretionary concessions beyond the defined contractual mechanisms (credits, MFN, benchmarking). Section 22.3 also indicates all fees are stated in the SOW baseline exhibits, and the Agreement avoids assumptions that re‑open pricing absent formal mechanisms (e.g., Benchmarking) .

### Please explain the reasonableness whether it is highly probable that a significant reversal in the amount of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur (ASC 606-10-32-11)

- Service credits: Use the most likely credit per period incorporating current performance data and SLA histories; recognize reductions only to the extent it is highly probable no significant reversal will occur.  
- Most‑Favored Pricing and Benchmarking: Do not pre‑recognize reductions absent trigger events or mutual agreement; constrain any estimated reductions to zero until conditions are met (Section 22.2; Section 7.2)  .  
- Gainshare: Constrain to zero until a mutually approved mechanism/SOW exists, with defined baselines and measurement (Section 15.4) .  
- Extraordinary Event pricing and RRCs/ARCs: Recognize variable amounts contemporaneously with usage/approved adjustments; do not anticipate long‑duration changes before approval/occurrence (Sections 39–40) .

### Please consider the following factors, which could increase the likelihood or the magnitude of a revenue reversal (ASC 606-10-32-12)

- Question: Is the amount of consideration highly susceptible to factors outside the Company’s influence (e.g., movement of a market or an index like commodity price index, subsequent sales of a good or service of the customer)?  
  - Answer: **Yes (partially).** MFN adjustments depend on Provider’s pricing to other parties; benchmarking outcomes depend on external market data; Extraordinary Events depend on customer business conditions (Sections 22.2, 7.2, 39)   .

- Question: Does the Company expect that the uncertainty about the amount of consideration is not expected to be resolved for a long period of time?  
  - Answer: **No.** SLA credits are resolved monthly; MFN triggers are evaluated when comparator deals occur; benchmarking timelines are defined; Extraordinary Event adjustments require SOW‑based implementation with defined phasing (Sections 6, 22.2, 7.2, 39.2)    .

- Question: Have the Company limited experience or other limited evidence with similar types of contracts resulting in limited predictive value?  
  - Answer: **No.** This Agreement is a continuation of a 2020–2024 relationship, now renewed effective April 1, 2024, indicating operational history to inform estimates (preamble; Section 1) .

- Question: Does the Company have a practice of offering price concessions or changing payment terms of similar contracts in similar circumstances?  
  - Answer: **No.** Pricing changes are governed by explicit contractual mechanisms (MFN, benchmarking, Extraordinary Events); payment terms are fixed (net 30) (Sections 22.2, 7.2, 39; 23.1)    .

- Question: Has the Company a practice of either offering a broad range of price concessions or changing the payment terms and conditions of similar contracts in similar circumstances?  
  - Answer: **No.** Changes occur only through the Agreement’s defined procedures; no broad discretionary concessions are evidenced (Sections 22.2, 7.2)  .

- Question: The transaction price consists of variable consideration(s) which are deemed probable and need(s) to be included in the transaction price.  
  - Answer: **Yes (in part).** Include usage-based variability and the most likely amount of SLA credits each period; constrain contingent items like gainshare, MFN retroactive reductions, and benchmarking adjustments to zero until triggers/agreements occur (Sections 6, 22.2, 7.2, 39–40)    .

## Conclusion

- The transaction price consists of fixed consideration per the rate structures in the BPO SOW (Exhibit B.1(c)) and variable consideration reflecting: (i) SLA Performance Credits, (ii) usage‑based volumes and RRC/ARC/Extraordinary Event adjustments, (iii) MFN price guarantees, and (iv) any mutually agreed benchmarking or gainsharing adjustments.  
- Provider should estimate service credits using the most likely amount each month and recognize volume‑driven consideration as services are provided.  
- Constrain uncertain/contingent items (MFN, benchmarking, gainshare) to zero until the relevant condition is met or mutual agreement is executed to avoid significant reversal (Sections 6, 22.2, 7.2, 39–40)    .

## B. Non-cash consideration

- Question: Does the consideration include a non-cash component?  
  - Answer: **No.** The Agreement contemplates cash payments via invoicing and net 30 settlement; no non‑cash consideration for the services is specified. The Innovation Fund is a Provider‑funded pool for initiatives (does not constitute non‑cash consideration received by Provider) (Sections 23.1, 3.15)  .

## C. Consideration payable to a customer:

Guidance: Consideration payable to a customer generally reduces the transaction price unless it is in exchange for a distinct good or service from the customer.

- Question: Is there any consideration payable to a customer (ASC 606-10-32-25, 32-26 and 32-27)?  
  - Answer: **Yes (in form of credits).** Service/Performance Credits and certain refunds (e.g., MFN retroactive refunds) function as amounts payable/creditable to the customer and reduce the transaction price; these are explicitly contemplated (Service Levels; Section 22.2)  .

Guidance: If variable, estimate and apply the constraint per ASC 606-10-32-5 to 32-9.

- Consideration Payable to customer:  
  - Performance Credits reduce amounts billed when SLAs are not achieved (Service Levels/Exhibit B.1(b); Section 6.4 references Performance Credits). MFN provisions require retroactive refunds where applicable (Section 22.2). Transition Milestone Credits can also apply during the Transition phase (Section 4.4(A))    .

- Question: Does the consideration payable to a customer (or to the customer's) represent a payable for a distinct goods or service?  
  - Answer: **No.** These credits/refunds are not for distinct goods/services promised by the customer to Provider; they are price adjustments or remedies that reduce the amount of consideration for Provider’s services (Service Credits, MFN refunds)  .

### Conclusion
- Treat service credits, MFN refunds, and other credits as reductions of the transaction price when the triggering conditions are met or when the most likely amount can be estimated without significant risk of reversal. No separate distinct services from the customer are received in exchange for these amounts.

  - Answer: **Yes (less than one year).** Provider invoices within 5 days after month‑end and payment is due on the 30th calendar day from the invoice date; no material prepayments are required. Therefore, the practical expedient applies and no significant financing component is present (Section 23.1) .
