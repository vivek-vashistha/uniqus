# **Step 2 – Identifying the Performance Obligations in a Contract**

The process of identifying performance obligations requires an entity to determine whether it promises to transfer either goods or services that are distinct, or a series of distinct goods and services that meet certain conditions.
These promises may not be limited to those explicitly included in written contracts.

---

## **Analyzing whether identified promises represent performance obligations**

### **Guidance**

A promise is classified as a performance obligation if the good or service is:

* capable of being distinct (ASC 606-10-25-19 (a)), and
* distinct in the context of the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19 (b)) (ASC 606-10-25-14).

---

### **Criterion 1**

Can the customer benefit from the goods or services (e.g., goods or services could be used, consumed, or sold for an amount that is greater than scrap value) on its own or together with other readily available resources (ASC 606-10-25-19(a))?

### **Criterion 2**

Is the company’s promise to transfer the good or service separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (ASC 606-10-25-19(b))?

Considerations:
i. Significant integration service
ii. Significant modification/customization
iii. Highly dependent/highly interrelated

## **Conclusion**

The contract contains **three series-type performance obligations** (Phone Support, Chat Support, and PSO), which are each a series of substantially similar services satisfied over time, and **two bundled/support obligations** (Training & Knowledge Support and Technology Reporting) that are not distinct from core services.
Additionally, the **Transition and Transformation services** represent a **distinct one-time performance obligation** satisfied at project milestones.

**Overall Conclusion:**

> The contract comprises multiple distinct and series performance obligations.
> Core service obligations (Phone, Chat, PSO) are recognized over time based on input measures (FTE hours and volume-based SLA).
> Transition/Transformation activities are recognized at point-in-time upon milestone acceptance.
> Support functions (Training, Reporting) are incidental and included within the transaction price allocation for core services.
