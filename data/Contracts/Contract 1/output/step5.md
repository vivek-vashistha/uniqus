# **Step 5 – Recognize Revenue When (or As) Each Performance Obligation Is Satisfied**

Under the Work Order, the Company (WNS) provides continuous, recurring service operations — inbound call handling, chat support, and back-office processing — which transfer benefits to the customer as the services are performed. Transition and Transformation activities are milestone-based and satisfied at a point in time when specific deliverables are completed and accepted.

### **Question:** Performance obligations satisfied over time?

**Answer:** **Yes.**
The core operational services — Phone, Chat, PSO, and Shared Services — satisfy criteria (a) and (c) of ASC 606-10-25-27 because the customer simultaneously receives and consumes benefits as the services are rendered. The contract structure (monthly invoicing, continuous SLAs) evidences ongoing transfer of control.

### **Question:** Performance obligations satisfied at a point in time?

**Answer:** **Yes (for Transition and Transformation obligations).**
These are milestone-based and recognized at the point when WNS completes and the customer formally accepts the deliverables (e.g., “soft go-live,” “full deployment,” or “solution acceptance” per Exhibit A, Tab 3 and Exhibit E).

## **Selecting a Method that Depicts the Transfer of Control**

### **Method 1: Output Method**

> Progress is measured based on the value of services delivered to the customer to date, as evidenced by achievement of SLA-based metrics and monthly performance reporting (e.g., calls handled, chat transactions completed, cases processed).

### **Method 2: Input Method**

> Progress is measured based on the Company’s efforts or inputs towards satisfying a performance obligation, relative to the total expected inputs (e.g., FTE hours consumed, process volumes handled, time elapsed, or costs incurred).

---

### **Guidance: Limitations on Applying Units-of-Delivery or Units-of-Production Methods**

An **output method** based purely on “units delivered” (e.g., number of calls handled) would **not faithfully depict performance** because:

> Some services (e.g., training, reporting, quality assurance) provide value continuously or cumulatively, even if not linked to discrete units delivered. Additionally, at the end of a reporting period, work-in-progress and partially completed services (e.g., cases in review or policy renewals in queue) are already under customer control but may not yet count as “units delivered.”

Therefore, **input-based progress measures** (such as time, FTE hours, or cost incurred) are applied to depict a faithful representation of transfer of control for ongoing service obligations.

---

✅ **Summary Conclusion**

> Revenue for ongoing operational services (Phone, Chat, PSO, Shared Services) is recognized **over time** based on monthly inputs and outputs reflecting continuous service delivery.
> Transition and Transformation revenues are recognized **at a point in time** upon milestone completion and customer acceptance.
> This approach faithfully represents the pattern of transfer of control consistent with ASC 606-10-25-27 and 25-32.
