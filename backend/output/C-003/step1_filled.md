# Step 1: Identifying contract with the customer

## A. Contract exists if

i. Collection is considered probable

ii. It has commercial substance

iii. Rights to goods and services and payment terms can be identified

iv. It is approved and the parties are committed to their obligations

### Existence of a contract

Description | Yes/No | Analysis
--- | --- | ---
Is the contract approved and the parties are committed to their obligation ((ASC 606-10-25-1(a) ) ? | **Yes** | The contract is signed by both parties, indicating approval and commitment.
Are each parties' rights regarding the goods or services to be transferred identifiable (ASC 606-10-25-1(b)) ? | **Yes** | The agreement specifies the services to be provided and the rights of both parties.
Can the payment terms for the goods and services be identified (ASC 606-10-25-1(c)) | **Yes** | Payment terms are clearly laid out, including service fees and payment schedules.
Has the contract commercial substance (ASC 606-10-25-1(d)) ? | **Yes** | The contract outlines services and fees which affect the cash flow of both parties, illustrating commercial substance.
Is the collection of consideration probable ("likely") (ASC 606-10-25-1(e)) ? | **Yes** | There are stipulations for payment and termination based on fulfillment of obligations, indicating a likelihood of collection.

Conclusion: A contract exists between the parties based on criteria met for approval, identifiable rights and obligations, and probability of payment.

## B. Combination of contracts

Accounted for as a single contract if one or more of the following criteria are met

• Contracts were negotiated as a single commercial package;

• Consideration in one contract depends on the other contract; or

• Goods and services are a single performance obligation
