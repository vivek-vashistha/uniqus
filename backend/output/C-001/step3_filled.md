# Step 3

Step 3: Determining transaction price

The 'transaction price' is the amount of consideration to which an entity expects to be entitled in exchange for transferring goods or services to a customer, excluding the amount collected on behalf of third parties - e.g. some sales taxes. To determine this amount, an entity considers multiple factors.

An entity estimates the transaction price at contract inception, including any variable consideration, and updates the estimate each reporting period for any changes in circumstances. When determining the transaction price, an entity assumes that the goods or services will be transferred to the customer based on the terms of the existing contract, and does not take into consideration the possibility of a contract being cancelled, renewed, or modified.

## Transaction price includes

## i Variable consideration (and the constraint)

The Company estimated the amount of variable consideration to which it expects to be entitled, giving consideration to the risk of revenue reversal in making an estimate.

## Ii Non-cash consideration

Non-cash consideration is measured at fair value, if it can be reasonably estimated, if not, an entity uses the stand-alone selling price of the good or service that was promised in exchange for noncash consideration.

Iii Significant financing component

For contracts with a significant financing component, an entity adjusts the promised amount of consideration to reflect the time value of money.

## Iv Consideration payable to the customer

An entity needs to determine whether consideration payable to a customer represents a reduction of the transaction price, a payment for a distinct goods or services or a combination of the two.

## C. Consideration payable to a customer: 

Guidance
Consideration payable to a customer includes cash amounts that an entity pays, or expects to pay, to the customer (or to other parties that purchase the Company' goods or services from the customer) and credits or other items (e.g. coupon or voucher) that can be applied against amounts owed to the Company (or to other parties that purchase the Company' goods or services from the customer). 		

- Question: Is there any consideration payable to a customer (ASC 606-10-32-25, 32-26 and 32-27)? 
  - Answer: **Yes** — The Work Order requires the Vendor to pay a signing bonus of $8,500,000 in December 2024 to offset the customer’s one-time transition costs; this is explicitly presented as a negative amount in the Annexure to Exhibit A (Tab 4) and described in Exhibit A Section I (Deal Bonus) .

Guidance		
If the consideration payable to a customer includes a variable amount, an entity shall estimate the transaction price (including assessing whether the estimate of variable consideration is constrained) in accordance with paragraphs ASC 606-10-32-5 to 32-1350–58

- Consideration Payable to customer:
  - The $8.5M signing bonus is consideration payable to the customer and therefore reduces the transaction price for the related services. Under ASC 606-10-32-25 to 32-27, reduce revenue at the later of (a) when the related services are transferred or (b) when the Company pays or promises to pay the consideration. The promise exists at contract inception, and the bonus is linked to offsetting 2024 transition costs; therefore, the reduction should be recognized as the related services are provided (with allocation across the related performance obligations), with any remaining amount addressed upon payment in December 2024 if necessary. Management should apply judgment to align the reduction to the period of benefit (e.g., Year 1 services) consistent with the stated purpose of offsetting 2024 transition costs .

- Question: Does the consideration payable to a customer (or to the customer's) represent a payable for a distinct goods or service ?
  - Answer: **No** — The bonus is not in exchange for a distinct good or service from the customer; it is to offset the customer’s one-time transition costs. Therefore, it is a reduction of the transaction price rather than consideration for a distinct deliverable .

### Conclusion
- The $8.5M signing bonus reduces the transaction price for the related services and should be recognized as a reduction of revenue consistent with ASC 606’s “later of” guidance, allocated to the related performance obligations.
